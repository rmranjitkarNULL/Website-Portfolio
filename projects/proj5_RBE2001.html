2-DoF Planar Manipulator

C/C++ SolidWorks MATLAB FEA Robotics Pololu Romi
Overview Design Analysis Control Results
// 01

Overview

A 2-DoF translational planar manipulator mounted on a Pololu Romi 32U4 mobile platform, designed to autonomously swap boxes across a three-level shelf. All structural components are custom 3D-printed ABS — linkages, gears, and bearing mounts — modeled and refined in SolidWorks.

A 20:1 worm-gear reduction provided actuation torque, while a pantograph linkage maintained gripper parallelism throughout the workspace. Each component underwent FEA to verify strength while minimizing weight.

// 02

Design & Fabrication

The manipulator uses a five-bar planar linkage driven by a DC motor with a 20:1 worm-gear stage and a servo motor with a 2:1 gear reduction. All links, mounts, and brackets were custom-modeled and 3D-printed in ABS for rapid prototyping and iteration.

Press-fit axles and ball bearings at each joint ensure low-friction motion and precise alignment. The pantograph-style parallelogram keeps the gripper level throughout the workspace, simplifying grasping and improving mechanical precision.

// 03

Analysis & Simulation

Using SolidWorks Simulation, FEA verified that critical links maintained safety factors above 2.0 under load. Free-body diagrams and MATLAB modeling calculated joint torques, optimized actuator sizing, and refined link geometry.

Additional tipping and stability calculations ensured the Romi platform remained balanced when the arm was fully extended. Iterative refinements — shortening upper links and thickening bushings — improved stiffness and reduced deflection.

// 04

Control & System Integration

An integrated control system synchronized actuation between the manipulator and mobile base. The Romi microcontroller coordinated servo and DC motor actuation through an inverse-kinematics–based position control framework.

Motion commands were transmitted via serial communication, enabling precise incremental control of both joint and base movement. The software emphasized reliability and simplicity, achieving smooth coordinated motion without overcomplicating the control logic.

// 05

Testing & Performance

The manipulator successfully performed all required tasks — navigating to the shelf, grasping and swapping boxes at multiple heights, and returning to starting position. The structure remained stable and deformation-free under continuous operation, validating FEA predictions.

Despite early challenges including servo overheating and voltage drops, iterative refinements to power delivery and linkage design produced a reliable prototype that met all project requirements and competition benchmarks.
